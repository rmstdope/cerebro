State 1 — a measurement that comes back negative

Say ratatui hosts the session fine, but killing it loses the scrollback that is the record of a pass. Two ways the document can carry that:

Record it and carry on

S4 Killing a session, and what is left

Result: no. When the child exits, the prototype's screen model is dropped with the pty and the pane goes blank. Emacs keeps the buffer because vterm-kill-buffer-on-exit is bound to nil (cerebro.el:3521) and a buffer is not a process.

Keeping the parsed screen after the child exits is possible — it is a Vec of rows the prototype currently drops — but it was not built here, and the cost of that is estimated, not measured: roughly one screen-buffer type plus a lifecycle distinct from the pty's, which today does not exist.

The bead comes back to you with a note saying the ratatui prototype could not keep a buffer past process exit, and asking whether to continue. Nothing is written to docs/. The other three measurements are in a scratch directory nobody reads.

My recommendation is the left one. A negative measurement is the most valuable thing this bead can produce — it is exactly the evidence a desk study cannot get — and a spike that stops at the first "no" delivers nothing at all.

State 2 — the evidence does not support a recommendation

Recommendation

Stay on Emacs, and revisit if libvterm becomes a problem. The ratatui route is buildable — S1, S2 and S3 all came back yes — but the migration is 6 267 lines of elisp and 497 ERT cases, and nothing measured here is something the current view does badly. The one real cost it removes is "the navigator must have Emacs and libvterm", which has cost this project nothing so far.

A recommendation to change nothing is a permitted, and quite likely, outcome — the document ranks all three, and staying is one of the three.
