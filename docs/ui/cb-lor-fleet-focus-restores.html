cb-lor — the surface the navigator approved, 2026-09-04

One round. This file is the specification for what happens to a bead pinned in the Session pane of cerebro-tui when focus arrives at the Fleet pane. It is the fourth of the navigation beads, beside cb-d31 (Enter into a session), cb-3v5 (Tab out of one) and cb-5kk (F1/F2/F3), and it agrees with all three. It supersedes two rows of docs/ui/cb-41r-bead-detail.html — the Tab/Shift-Tab row of its §3 and the ↑/↓-under-Fleet row of its §4, both of which said the pinned bead survives. Everything else that file decided stands, and this one changes nothing any cb-kcs file decided: not a fleet row, not a lifecycle key, not a pane's borders, not the priority keys, not Enter on a +N more row.

1. The rule

The moment focus lands on the Fleet pane, a pinned bead is dropped and the Session pane draws the selected agent again — its live session, one starting, its retained pass, its refused launch, or its ordinary empty body. Every way in does it: Tab from Session, Shift-Tab from Work, and F1 from anywhere. There is then no state in which Fleet is focused and a bead is pinned, so the arrow keys under Fleet never meet one and there is nothing left to decide about them.

Rejected: Fleet focus as a glance — the bead covered rather than dropped, and drawn again on the way back to the Session pane at the offset it had. It makes looking at the fleet free, at the price of a title hint ([Tab twice for cb-a54]) without which the rule is unlearnable, a second rule for Enter under Fleet, and this becoming the only pane in the view whose content depends on which pane is focused. Also rejected: the selection rather than the focus — arriving at Fleet leaves the bead alone and only ↑/↓ drops it, which answers half the complaint and leaves a navigator who tabs over, looks, and tabs back still holding a bead over the agent they were just looking at. And: a dismissal key. The return is implicit by request, and Esc already quits.

Nothing is announced. No gold notice, no clause in the header — the pane changing is the feedback, and a notice would fire on every Tab round the cycle and be cleared by the next keystroke anyway. The Work cursor has not moved, so Shift-Tab then Enter puts the same bead back.

The pane's ordinary empty body, word for word as it already reads — the bead is not left in place as something better to draw. On a read-only view it is that view's own two lines instead (Sessions are hosted by the view that supervises this / checkout. This one does not.), unchanged. Rejected: keeping the bead when there is no session to show, which would make the same keystroke do two different things depending on the agent.

6. Getting the bead back

Enter on its row in the Work pane, which pins it again, moves focus to the Session pane and re-reads it — exactly as opening it the first time. It opens at the top: a dropped bead keeps nothing, and neither does the Session pane's scroll offset, which returns to zero on every drop so a retained transcript is never handed the offset the bead was left at. The navigator who was three hundred lines into a plan pays for the glance at the fleet; that is the accepted cost of the one rule in §1.

7. Two rules carried over rather than decided

The keyboard. A pinned bead never gives the child the keyboard, so a Tab pressed over one was never going to reach an agent and does not begin to now.
The lease. Moving focus is the navigator's own act and touches no session, so this works on a read-only view exactly as it does on a supervising one — like x, like the priority keys, like cb-d31's Enter.
